Hey, I'm Richard. I'm a Product Designer based in Toronto, Canada.

Right now, I'm at CIBC — working across complex user flows and design systems in one of the more demanding design environments out there: financial services. Previously, I've designed and researched at RBC Insurance, Symcor Inc and across other high-stakes, regulation-heavy domains. The kind of work most designers avoid — messy, ambiguous, deeply systemic — is honestly where I do my best thinking.

Much of my work lives at the intersection of complexity and clarity. Taking something that feels impossible to navigate and making it feel obvious to the person using it. I come from a psychology background, which shapes how I approach everything. I'm less interested in what users do and more interested in why — and I think that distinction matters more than most teams realize.

My working style is pretty deliberate: I break problems apart before I try to solve them, share rough ideas early to force real reactions, and treat data as a flashlight — useful for finding where something's broken, not for telling you how to fix it.

Lately, I've been thinking a lot about AI — specifically the gap between the capital being poured into it and the returns that have yet to materialize (IMO). As a designer, that tension feels important. The tools are moving fast, but the experiences still have a long way to go.

Outside of work, I'm usually on my road bike, on a mountain somewhere snowboarding, or deep in a rabbit hole about markets and human behaviour. Find me on LinkedIn or reach out via email richardhuynh0202@gmail.com
